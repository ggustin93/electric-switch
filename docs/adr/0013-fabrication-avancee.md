# 0013 — Early replacement: brought-forward manufacturing as the low estimate (revised: not used in the verdict)

Status: accepted (2026-10-13), after a data-science review, an LCA review and three research passes (`docs/research/2026-10-remplacement-anticipe.md`, including a `/deep-research` run with 3-vote adversarial checks).

## Context
« Ma décision » compares, over h = the years the current car would still run, keeping it against replacing it now. A new EV carried 100 % of its manufacturing (H3) against only h years of use. The owner objected that after h years a car would be bought anyway, so replacing now only brings that manufacturing forward.
- Lenski et al. 2010 (eq. 3) and the ICCT (Bieker & Mock 2020, p. 7–8, 13–14) count the brought-forward share, ≈ manufacturing × h / L.
- Payback studies (Kagawa 2013, Campbell & Geyer 2026, the DG CLIMA scrappage model) count 100 %, and ADEME's retrofit study (2021, p. 8) says that 100 % penalises the scrap-and-buy-an-EV scenario.
- The literature is split and the two conventions can flip a verdict; with h / L alone, 54 of 72 new-EV test verdicts flip, mostly to « remplacer » in Belgium and France.

## Decision
- **Model:** `decider()` computes, for a new EV only, `partAvancee = min(1, horizon / min(VIE_ANS, VIE_KM / kmAn))` and `tRemplacerBas`. The verdict is « remplacer » if replacing wins even at 100 %, « garder » if keeping wins even with the brought-forward share, otherwise « serré » with `selonProchaine` when the two bounds disagree. Used EVs keep H2 (ADR 0012); `kmBascule(params, bas)` gives either threshold.
- **UI** (final, 2026-10-13, after two UX and three scientific reviews and a `/deep-research` check, research note § 8): when `selonProchaine`, the verdict uses the cautious bound (100 %) by default, because most households buy used and new BEVs are a minority of purchases. A visible line states the assumption and a « Voir ce calcul » link switches to the brought-forward share (« Revenir au calcul prudent » switches back); verdict, EV column and mileage threshold follow. The scrapping note shows under « remplacer ». Superseded first design: when `selonProchaine`, the verdict card asks « Dans 5 ans, votre voiture sera usée. Achèteriez-vous alors une électrique de toute façon ? » (Oui / Non / Je ne sais pas, default « Je ne sais pas »). « Oui » uses the brought-forward share, « Non » 100 %, and the verdict and the EV column follow the answer; unanswered, the title says what it depends on, the text gives « Si oui… / Si non… » and the column shows the range. The mileage threshold follows the answer. A note says the current car is assumed scrapped.
- **Méthode / Questions:** H3 describes both estimates and cites Lenski 2010 and ICCT 2020 (sources 26, 27); the `conventions` note states the scrapping assumption instead of the unsourced « effet global à peu près nul »; Q2 and Q4 give both thresholds.

## Consequences
- Many short- and medium-horizon cases with a new EV move from « garder » to « cela dépend de votre prochaine voiture »; « remplacer » and the Questions thresholds at 100 % are unchanged.
- The bias ADEME acknowledges is no longer hidden in the verdict, without claiming that early replacement always pays off.
- Revisit if a source gives a household-level substitution rate, or if the old car's resale is modelled.
- **Current car's fate** (amended 2026-10-13, three scientific reviews and a UX rewrite): the calculation does not assume scrapping, only that the current car's use stops. Resold, it may push an older car off the road (gain close) or be exported or added to the fleet (gain shrinks, may vanish); no source says which is more frequent, and the 13–16 % leakage of Jacobsen & van Benthem (2015) measures a fleet-wide price effect of fuel standards, not this case, so it is not cited. Scrapping a working car is never presented as the reference. The owner chose to keep it off the verdict card, to keep the card light: it is stated in « Comment lire ce résultat » (just under the verdict), H3 and À propos. The UX trust review noted the risk that a sceptical reader misses it, since the assumption only flatters « remplacer ».

## Revision (2026-10-13, owner's objection, four experts, 3 of 4)
The « Voir ce calcul » view combined two favourable assumptions: only the brought-forward share of the new EV is counted, **and** the current car's fuel disappears. Lenski's equation 3 holds for cars destroyed by the scheme; a car with years left is usually resold and keeps running, in which case the new EV adds a car to the fleet (back towards 100 %) and the fuel saving only partly happens. On the default profile the brought-forward view only wins if at least 44 % of the old car's use truly disappears, a share no source measures. The owner found the view unclear.
- **Decision:** the verdict always uses the cautious estimate (100 % for a new EV), as in production before this ADR. The brought-forward share is described in H3 as an alternative reading with both conditions (next car a comparable new EV anyway, current car off the road), not computed. Questions Q2 and Q4 return to their production wording. Model fields `partAvancee`, `tRemplacerBas` and `selonProchaine` are removed.
- Under « remplacer », the card keeps a light « Hypothèses du calcul : H3 » link.
